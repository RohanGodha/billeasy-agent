# Payments & Compliance — GST, RBI Payment Aggregator, Settlement, MDR, NCMC & DPDP (Reference Knowledge Base)

> Reference knowledge for the Counter Copilot assistant used by Billeasy Area Partner
> Managers. Covers the rules that govern a counter's digital bill, its settlement and the
> messages we send its supervisor. Figures are representative of Indian payments and GST
> norms and must be confirmed against the latest CBIC notification, RBI circular or NPCI
> Operating Circular before any commitment is made to a merchant or transit authority.
>
> Where a number is a **Billeasy modelling convention** rather than a statutory rule, it is
> labelled **[product convention]**. Never present a product convention to a partner as law.

## 1. GST e-invoicing — who must generate an IRN

e-Invoicing means reporting a **B2B** invoice to a government Invoice Registration Portal
(IRP), which returns an **IRN** and a digitally signed QR code. Only then is the invoice a
valid tax document.

| Aggregate Annual Turnover (AATO) in any FY from 2017-18 | e-invoicing applicable from |
|---|---|
| Above ₹500 crore | 1 Oct 2020 |
| Above ₹100 crore | 1 Jan 2021 |
| Above ₹50 crore | 1 Apr 2021 |
| Above ₹20 crore | 1 Apr 2022 |
| Above ₹10 crore | 1 Oct 2022 |
| **Above ₹5 crore** | **1 Aug 2023 — the current entry threshold** |

Key points an Area Manager must get right:

1. e-Invoicing applies to **B2B supplies, exports, SEZ supplies and deemed exports**. It does
   **not** apply to B2C invoices — a shopper at a counter or a ferry passenger does not
   trigger an IRN.
2. Once a taxpayer crosses the threshold in **any** financial year from 2017-18 onwards, the
   obligation is permanent — turnover falling back below ₹5 crore does not switch it off.
3. Exempt categories include banks and NBFCs, insurers, goods transport agencies,
   **passenger transportation services**, cinema admission and SEZ units.
4. **Reporting window:** taxpayers with AATO of ₹10 crore and above must report an invoice to
   the IRP within **30 days of the invoice date**; older invoices are rejected by the portal.
   The window was first notified for the ₹100 crore-plus band and later widened — confirm the
   band in force.
5. An IRN can be **cancelled on the IRP within 24 hours** only, and cannot be partially
   cancelled. After 24 hours the correction route is a **credit note or debit note**, plus an
   amendment in GSTR-1.

**[product convention]** Counter Copilot's `gst_threshold_crossed` feature fires at **monthly
TPV ≥ ₹5,00,000** (≈ ₹60 lakh annualised). That is a *prospecting* trigger for
`MOD-BILLING` — a counter at that volume is well past the GST **registration** thresholds
and should be issuing compliant bills. It is **not** the ₹5 crore e-invoicing threshold and
must never be described to a merchant as one.

## 2. IRN, the signed QR code and the B2C dynamic-QR mandate

**IRN (Invoice Reference Number):** a 64-character hash computed from **supplier GSTIN +
document type + document number + financial year**. It is unique per invoice, generated by
the IRP (not by the seller), and returned with a digitally signed JSON and a signed QR code.

The **signed QR code on a B2B e-invoice** carries supplier GSTIN, recipient GSTIN, invoice
number and date, invoice value, line-item count, main HSN and the IRN — it lets any officer
verify the invoice offline.

**The B2C dynamic QR mandate is a different rule.** Under Notification 14/2020-Central Tax
(effective 1 Dec 2020, penalty waived until 30 Sep 2021), a registered person with
**aggregate turnover above ₹500 crore** must display a **Dynamic QR code** on **B2C
invoices** that enables the buyer to pay digitally. Key clarifications (Circular 156/12/2021-GST):

- If the buyer pays through **UPI, a card or any prescribed electronic mode** and the invoice
  carries the transaction reference, the dynamic-QR requirement is treated as met — the payment
  QR at the counter effectively satisfies it.
- **Exempt suppliers:** insurers, banking companies and financial institutions/NBFCs, goods
  transport agencies, **passenger transportation services**, cinema exhibition and OIDAR
  suppliers. Most transit ticketing counters therefore sit outside this mandate.
- A **static** QR that requires the customer to type the amount does **not** satisfy the rule
  where the mandate applies.

**[product convention]** `MOD-QR` (Dynamic QR Collect) is sold as a **collections and
leakage-control** product, not as a GST compliance product. Do not pitch it as "required by
the GST dynamic QR mandate" unless the merchant is genuinely above ₹500 crore turnover.

## 3. HSN / SAC codes a counter will actually use

HSN (goods) and SAC (services) reporting on invoices, per Notification 78/2020-Central Tax
from 1 April 2021:

| Aggregate Annual Turnover | Digits required |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹5 crore | **4 digits** on B2B invoices (optional on B2C) |
| Above ₹5 crore | **6 digits** on all invoices |

Service codes that appear on transit ticketing counters (chapter heading **9964 — passenger
transport services**):

| SAC | Service |
|---|---|
| 996411 | Local land transport of passengers by railway, metro, monorail, bus, tramway, auto, three-wheeler |
| 996412 | Taxi services, including radio taxi |
| 996413 | Non-scheduled local bus and coach charter |
| 996414 | Other land transport of passengers |
| **996415** | **Local water transport of passengers — ferries, cruises** |
| 996416 | Sightseeing transportation |
| 996419 | Other local passenger transport |

Retail counters use goods HSN (e.g. 1006 rice, 1701 sugar, 3401 soap, 2202 aerated
beverages). Many public passenger-transport services are **exempt or nil-rated** (metro rail,
non-AC stage carriage, inland-waterway passenger transport), while AC stage carriage is
commonly taxed at 5% without ITC.

**[product convention]** Counter Copilot **does not compute tax liability or rate**. It flags
whether a compliant bill was *issued*. Rate and exemption questions go to the merchant's own
tax advisor or Billeasy's finance desk.

## 4. Credit notes, debit notes and voided tickets

1. A **credit note** under **Section 34 of the CGST Act** is issued when the taxable value or
   tax charged was excessive, goods were returned, or the service was deficient.
2. A credit note must be **declared no later than 30 November following the end of the
   financial year** in which the supply was made, or the date of filing the annual return,
   whichever is earlier. After that the tax cannot be adjusted.
3. **Credit notes for e-invoices must themselves be reported to the IRP** where e-invoicing
   applies to the supplier.
4. A credit note **reduces the supplier's output tax only if** the recipient has reversed the
   corresponding input tax credit.
5. **Voided ticket ≠ credit note.** A ticket voided at the counter before the fare is settled
   is an operational reversal in the AFC/POS system. Once the fare has been reported and
   settled, the correction is a refund plus a credit note.
6. **Why this matters to leakage:** a pattern of `void_reissue` rows with no matching refund
   or credit note is the classic signature of *issue-void-pocket-the-cash-reissue* fraud. See
   `counter_performance_methodology.md` §4.

## 5. RBI Payment Aggregator / Payment Gateway framework

Source framework: **Guidelines on Regulation of Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways**
(DPSS.CO.PD.No.1810/02.14.008/2019-20, 17 March 2020, amended 31 March 2021), read with the
Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Who is regulated | **Payment Aggregators (PAs)** — they onboard merchants and handle funds. Must be authorised by RBI under the PSS Act |
| Who is not | **Payment Gateways (PGs)** — pure technology routers that never touch funds; treated as outsourcing partners |
| Net worth at application | **₹15 crore** |
| Net worth to be reached | **₹25 crore by the end of the third financial year** of authorisation, and maintained thereafter |
| Fund handling | Funds must sit in an **escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank**; a second escrow with another bank is permitted |
| Merchant onboarding | Board-approved policy; background and **KYC checks on every merchant** per the RBI Master Direction on KYC |
| Card data | PAs and merchants **must not store card credentials**; only tokens, plus last four digits and issuer name, since 1 Oct 2022 |
| Security | Baseline security controls, PCI-DSS scope, no ATM PIN as an authentication factor for card-not-present |
| Grievance redress | Published policy, named nodal/grievance officer, resolution timelines, escalation to the RBI Ombudsman scheme |

RBI has extended this framework to **physical / offline point-of-sale acquiring (PA-P)** so
that offline aggregators face the same authorisation, net-worth and escrow discipline as
online ones, and separately regulates **cross-border PAs (PA-CB)**. Confirm the current
consolidated directions and applicability dates before quoting them to a partner.

## 6. Nodal versus escrow accounts — how counter money is held

**Nodal account (legacy).** Under the 2009 intermediary directions, an intermediary collected
customer money into a **nodal account** with a bank — an internal account of the bank, not of
the intermediary — with settlement to merchants within the prescribed cycle (commonly quoted
as T+3). The intermediary had no proprietary claim on the money.

**Escrow account (current).** A non-bank PA must maintain an **escrow account** with a
scheduled commercial bank. The bank is the escrow agent; the balance is not the PA's money and
is not available to its creditors.

**Permitted credits into the escrow:**
1. Payments from customers for a purchase or a fare.
2. Pre-funding by the merchant or the PA (a PA may pre-fund from its own account).
3. Transfers representing successful settlements or refunds returned by merchants.

**Permitted debits from the escrow:**
1. Payment to merchants.
2. Refunds and chargeback returns to customers.
3. The PA's own commission, moved out to its operating account, **not later than T+1** from
   the settlement.
4. Amounts payable to sponsor banks / partners under the settlement construct.

**Hard rules:** the escrow balance may **not** be used as working capital, may not be
pledged, and may not be deployed for any purpose beyond the permitted debits. Escrow
operations are audited and reported to RBI, and the merchant's payable is a settlement
liability recognised against the escrow — it is never credit extended to the merchant.

**For government transit counters:** collections at a jetty, depot or metro station are
**public fare revenue**. They flow through the same escrow discipline and are settled to the
transit authority's **designated revenue account** under the agency agreement. Never suggest
netting, holding back or adjusting fare revenue at counter level.

## 7. T+1 settlement — what it means and when it slips

**T+1** means funds captured on day T are credited to the merchant or transit authority on
the **next settlement day**. Under the PA framework the settlement clock is defined by the
event, not by the calendar:

| Reference | Event that starts the clock | Settlement |
|---|---|---|
| **Ts** | Date the bank intimates the PA about the charge / credit to escrow | Ts + 1 |
| **Tp** | Date of charge or debit to the customer's account, where the PA pre-funds | Tp + 1 |
| **Td** | Date the delivery of goods is confirmed, where settlement is delivery-versus-payment | Td + 1 |

Practical realities the Area Manager should be able to explain:

1. **T+1 counts settlement days.** Sundays, second and fourth Saturdays, and bank holidays
   push the credit out — a Friday evening ferry rush usually lands on Monday or Tuesday.
2. **Capture cut-off matters.** A tap after the day's cut-off belongs to the next T. A late
   evening counter routinely sees "yesterday's" money on the day after tomorrow.
3. **NEFT and RTGS run 24x7**, so the payout leg itself is rarely the bottleneck; the
   reconciliation leg is.
4. **UPI settles between banks in multiple NPCI cycles per day**, but the merchant's credit
   still follows the PA's own T+1 cycle.
5. **Card acquiring** commonly settles T+1 or T+2 depending on network, MCC and risk profile.
6. **Offline / NCMC taps settle only once the device uploads its batch.** A gate that stayed
   offline for four hours has not "lost" the fares, but it has not reported them either —
   they cannot settle until the batch reaches the back office.

**[product convention]** The `settlement_cycle` field on a counter (`T+1` / `T+2` / `weekly`)
is a **commercial term in that counter's contract**, not a regulatory ceiling. A `weekly`
cycle typically indicates a transit-authority revenue-sharing arrangement with consolidated
remittance. Aging beyond the contracted cycle is what `pending_settlement` measures.

## 8. MDR rules and what zero MDR means for a counter's economics

**MDR (Merchant Discount Rate)** is the fee a merchant pays on a digital acceptance
transaction, shared between issuer (interchange), network and acquirer.

| Instrument | MDR position |
|---|---|
| **UPI (P2M)** | **Zero. Statutorily nil since 1 Jan 2020** |
| **RuPay debit card** | **Zero. Statutorily nil since 1 Jan 2020** |
| Other debit cards — merchant turnover up to ₹20 lakh | Capped at **0.40%**, max ₹200 per transaction (0.30% for QR-based) |
| Other debit cards — merchant turnover above ₹20 lakh | Capped at **0.90%**, max ₹1,000 per transaction (0.80% for QR-based) |
| Credit cards | **Not regulated** — market-determined, commonly ~1.5%–2.5% |
| Prepaid wallets / PPI | Contractual, commonly ~0.5%–2% |
| GST on MDR | **18%**, charged on the fee |

Legal basis for zero MDR: **Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007** and
**Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961**, with CBDT Circular 32/2019 prescribing
BHIM-UPI, UPI QR, RuPay debit, Aadhaar Pay, NEFT and RTGS as the prescribed modes on which no
charge — MDR included — may be levied. **Section 269SU** requires a business with turnover
above **₹50 crore** to offer these modes; **Section 271DB** penalises failure at **₹5,000 per
day**. Government **UPI incentive schemes** partially compensate acquirers for low-value
small-merchant P2M transactions, but the outlay is approved year to year and must not be
promised to a merchant as recurring income.

### What this means at a counter

1. As a counter's mix shifts from cash and credit card to **UPI and RuPay/NCMC**, the
   **acquiring revenue on those legs falls to zero.** Volume growth alone does not grow
   acquiring income.
2. Billeasy's **`take_rate` per module (§3 of the platform catalogue) is a platform
   commission for software and reconciliation — it is not MDR** and is not affected by the
   zero-MDR rule. Say this precisely: merchants confuse the two constantly.
3. Therefore the economics of a high-UPI counter rest on **module attach** — billing,
   reconciliation, e-ticketing, analytics, loyalty — not on transaction acquiring.
4. A merchant who says "UPI is free, why am I being charged?" is right about MDR and wrong
   about the platform fee. The honest framing: *zero MDR on the payment, a platform fee for
   the bill, the ticket, the reconciliation and the payout*.
5. **Never offer a rebate on a zero-MDR instrument** — there is nothing to rebate, and
   promising one creates a compliance problem, not a discount.
6. Cash looks free to a merchant but carries handling, shrinkage, remittance and audit cost —
   plus, at a transit counter, it is where the leakage lives.

## 9. NCMC / One Nation One Card and AFC gate specifications

The **National Common Mobility Card (NCMC)**, launched in March 2019 under the *One Nation One
Card* programme, is an **open-loop, EMV-contactless prepaid transit card** built on the RuPay
contactless specification, with an offline stored-value purse and operator "service area" data.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Card technology | Dual-interface EMV contactless, **ISO/IEC 14443 Type A/B at 13.56 MHz** |
| Stored value | Offline purse on the card; validated at the gate without a network round trip |
| Operating range | Typically ~2–4 cm tap distance |
| Gate transaction time | Target **under ~300 ms**, tap-to-open within ~500 ms |
| Gate throughput | Roughly **30–45 passengers per minute** per aisle at design load |
| Reader / gate | Indigenous **SWEEKAR** AFC reader and **SWAGAT** automatic fare gate (C-DAC), alongside vendor AFC systems |
| Fare models | **Distance-based** (tap in and tap out — metro), **flat fare** (single tap — bus, ferry) |
| Interoperability | One card across metro, bus, ferry, parking and retail acceptance |
| Top-up | Online (bank/app) or at station counters and TVMs; offline purse reloaded only in an authenticated online session |

**Offline payment limits.** NCMC's offline purse operates under RBI's framework for **offline
digital payments** (introduced January 2022), which set a per-transaction cap and a cumulative
cap between authenticated top-ups — originally **₹500 per transaction and ₹2,000 cumulative**,
subsequently enhanced (UPI Lite, for example, moved to ₹1,000 per transaction with a ₹5,000
balance). Confirm the limits currently in force before quoting them.

**Why an Area Manager cares:**

1. Offline validation means the **gate opens before the back office knows**. Fare records
   arrive in a **batch upload**, so an unreconciled gap is normal for minutes and abnormal for
   hours.
2. A **low `ncmc_share` at a high-velocity transit counter** means passengers are still buying
   paper or QR tickets at a window — queue time, cash handling and leakage all rise. That is
   the `MOD-ETICKET` case.
3. A **high `ncmc_share`** already captures the fare digitally, so the upside from e-ticketing
   is smaller — which is exactly why the propensity model weights `ncmc_share` **negatively**
   for `MOD-ETICKET`.
4. **Tap-out failures** in distance-based systems produce maximum-fare deductions and passenger
   disputes; these are resolved by the operator's AFC back office, not by a card chargeback.

## 10. DPDP Act 2023 — consent rules governing WhatsApp outreach

The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** governs every WhatsApp nudge Counter
Copilot drafts, because a supervisor's or owner's phone number is **personal data even in a
business relationship**.

| Concept | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Data Principal** | The counter supervisor, outlet owner or depot in-charge whose number we hold |
| **Data Fiduciary** | Billeasy — it decides why and how that data is processed |
| **Data Processor** | The messaging provider that delivers the WhatsApp template |
| **Consent Manager** | A registered entity through which a principal may give, manage and withdraw consent |
| **Significant Data Fiduciary** | A higher-obligation class notified by the Government (DPIA, audit, resident DPO) |

Consent rules that bind outreach:

1. Consent must be **free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, with a clear
   affirmative action**, and limited to the stated purpose.
2. It must be preceded by an **itemised notice** in plain language, available in English and
   the Eighth Schedule languages, saying what data, for what purpose, and how to withdraw and
   complain.
3. **Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent.** An opted-out supervisor must stop
   receiving messages, and the downstream processor must stop too.
4. **Purpose limitation:** a number collected for settlement alerts may not be repurposed into
   an unrelated marketing campaign without fresh consent.
5. **Data minimisation and erasure:** keep only what the purpose needs, and erase when the
   purpose is served or consent is withdrawn.
6. **Breach notification** to the Data Protection Board and to affected principals is mandatory.
7. **Penalties** run up to **₹250 crore** for failure to take reasonable security safeguards,
   up to **₹200 crore** for failure to notify a breach, and up to **₹50 crore** for other
   breaches of duty.
8. The Act is in force with rules notified and obligations phasing in — confirm the applicable
   compliance date rather than assuming.

Alongside DPDP, commercial messaging is also bound by **TRAI's TCCCP Regulations, 2018**
(DLT registration of the sender, header and template registration, consent registration and
scrubbing) and by the **WhatsApp Business Platform policy**: an opt-in is required before a
business-initiated message; templates are categorised as **utility, authentication or
marketing**; there is a **24-hour customer service window** after a user replies; and pricing
is now levied **per message** by category. Check the current rate card before quoting cost.

**[product convention] — Counter Copilot's own outreach rules:**
- Message only the supervisor or owner phone captured at onboarding, and only where the
  consent flag on the counter record is set.
- Use **utility-category templates** for settlement, device and compliance notices; never dress
  a promotion as a utility notice.
- Honour STOP/opt-out immediately and record it against the counter.
- The numeric compliance validator blocks any TPV, settlement or mismatch figure not supported
  by the counter's source data — an unsupported number is treated as a blocking failure, not a
  style issue.
- Messages stay **≤ 65 words**, are signed off with the Area Manager's first name, and state
  the specific action asked of the counter.

## 11. Twenty quick facts for fast recall

1. **GST e-invoicing threshold: AATO above ₹5 crore**, in force since **1 Aug 2023**.
2. e-Invoicing covers **B2B, exports, SEZ and deemed exports** — **not B2C**.
3. **IRN** is a **64-character hash** of supplier GSTIN + document type + document number +
   financial year, generated by the **IRP**.
4. An **IRN can be cancelled within 24 hours** only; after that, use a credit note.
5. Invoices must be reported to the IRP within **30 days** for the notified turnover band.
6. **B2C dynamic QR** is mandatory only above **₹500 crore** turnover; **passenger transport
   is exempt**.
7. **HSN digits:** 4 for AATO up to ₹5 crore, **6 above ₹5 crore**.
8. **SAC 996415** = local water transport of passengers (ferries); **996411** covers metro and
   bus passenger transport.
9. **Credit notes** must be declared by **30 November** following the end of the financial year.
10. **GST registration thresholds:** ₹40 lakh for goods and ₹20 lakh for services (₹20 lakh /
    ₹10 lakh in special category states).
11. **PA net worth: ₹15 crore** at application, **₹25 crore** by the end of the third financial
    year.
12. PA customer funds sit in an **escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank**; a second
    escrow with another bank is permitted.
13. A PA must move **its own commission out of escrow within T+1**.
14. Settlement runs on a **T+1** basis from **Ts / Tp / Td** as defined in the PA guidelines.
15. **MDR is zero on UPI (P2M) and RuPay debit** — Section 10A of the PSS Act read with
    Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act.
16. Other debit-card MDR is capped at **0.40% (≤₹200)** for merchants up to ₹20 lakh turnover
    and **0.90% (≤₹1,000)** above it; **credit card MDR is unregulated**.
17. **Section 271DB penalty: ₹5,000 per day** for not offering the prescribed electronic modes.
18. **NCMC** is an open-loop **EMV contactless** card on **ISO/IEC 14443 at 13.56 MHz**, with an
    offline purse and a gate target under ~**300 ms**.
19. **Contactless card payments without PIN** are permitted up to **₹5,000 per transaction**
    (raised from ₹2,000 with effect from 1 Jan 2021).
20. **DPDP penalties** reach **₹250 crore** for security-safeguard failures; **consent
    withdrawal must be as easy as consent**.
